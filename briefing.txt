=== SLIDE 2 ===
THE BUSINESS STORY
Who you are building this model for
A mid-size private bank receives about 3,000 personal loan applications a month. Every one gets a 25-minute manual review, and nearly half end up declined — many for reasons visible in the first minute. The Head of Retail Lending has asked the Credit Policy Analyst: can a model pre-screen applications so analysts spend their time where judgement is needed?
She needs two things from you:
1.  A model the credit team can trust
What drives decisions today? How well can a model predict them? Where does it fail?
2.  A threshold the bank can defend
How many applications can be auto-declined while wrongly declining no more than 5% of good customers — and what is that worth in analyst hours?

=== SLIDE 3 ===
"The day you understand the value of the work you do,
is the day you add more value to the work you do."
What this means for this use case:
A confusion matrix is not a score. Every false positive is a creditworthy customer turned away. Students should be able to say how many.
The threshold is a business decision. The bank sets the risk limit; the model only tells you where that limit falls.
Fairness is part of the job, not an add-on. Leaving gender out of the model does not, on its own, make the model fair.

=== SLIDE 5 ===
CBIM PROBLEM CANVAS
Before any code — 15 to 20 minutes
Complete the canvas before touching the data. This is not optional.
Situation (S)
~3,000 applications a month, each reviewed manually for ~25 minutes.
Complication (C)
Analyst time spent on clear-cut declines; good applicants wait longer.
Question (Q)
Not "build a model" — which applications can safely leave manual review, and at what threshold?
SSOT
The dataset — and the decision column is past analyst judgement, not repayment.
Success definition
A threshold within the 5% risk limit and a stated number of hours freed.
Stakeholder
Credit Policy Analyst — and the analysts and applicants affected.
